About Elections & Democracy in South Africa

South Africa has a complex history shaped by the apartheid era, where the majority of citizens were excluded from democratic participation. Under apartheid, political rights and voting were restricted based on race, leading to widespread inequality and international isolation.

In 1994, South Africa transitioned into a democratic state with its first fully inclusive elections, marking the end of apartheid and the beginning of a new constitutional democracy. Since then, elections have become a cornerstone of the country’s governance, ensuring that all eligible citizens have the right to vote freely and fairly.

This system aims to modernize the democratic process by improving transparency, security, and accessibility through digital registration and identity verification technologies.
